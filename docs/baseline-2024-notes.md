# 2024 baseline: derivation notes

[baseline_2024.json](../scenarios/baseline_2024.json) follows the method in [updating-the-baseline.md](updating-the-baseline.md). All values are for calendar year 2024 unless noted. Every input is generated by `baseline/build.py`; [baseline/reports/baseline_2024.md](../baseline/reports/baseline_2024.md) lists each source cell and [baseline/open-items.md](../baseline/open-items.md) tracks known issues. Sources: DUKES 2026 (2024 columns), ECUK 2025 end use tables (2024), final UK territorial greenhouse gas emissions 1990 to 2024, NTS 2024, TSGB 2025, DfT vehicle licensing statistics.

## Population and households

Population 69,256,000 (ONS mid 2024, as revised in the mid 2025 release). Households 28,609,000 (ONS families and households, 2025 edition, 2024 estimate). Both are generated by `baseline/build.py`.

## Electricity supply

The supply section is generated by `baseline/build.py`. See [baseline/reports/baseline_2024.md](../baseline/reports/baseline_2024.md) for every source cell. Capacities from DUKES 6.2 (offshore includes 0.08 GW floating), nuclear from 5.7. Observed 2024 capacity factors are 6.2 generation over end of year capacity times 8766 hours: offshore 34.5% (48.8 TWh), onshore 24.5% (34.8 TWh), solar 8.8% (15.0 TWh). The DUKES 6.3 load factors (36.0%, 25.0%, 9.2%) use average capacity over the year and would overstate generation. The offshore, onshore and solar availabilities are calibrated against the model so each reproduces its observed capacity factor (solar 0.933, which corrects the dataset's 9.4% to 8.8%). Hydro capacity factor 0.344 and nuclear 0.787 are generation over capacity times 8766 hours, reproducing 5.7 and 40.6 TWh.

Net interconnector imports, 33.4 TWh in 2024 (DUKES 1.1 electricity imports less exports), are dispatched with backup generation: each hour imports meet a share of the backup requirement (`electric_backup.prc_imports`, calibrated to 20.65%), up to the 9.84 GW of interconnector capacity in DUKES 5.13A. Imports therefore arrive when wind and solar are low. Before imports were modelled, backup generation filled the gap and exceeded observed dispatchable generation by roughly that amount. Modelled backup generation is now 128.4 TWh against 137.6 TWh observed.

`solarthermal_capacity` is calibrated so modelled solar thermal heat equals DUKES 6.1 final consumption of solar thermal (0.41 TWh), giving 0.5 GW. The same heat is part of water heating demand.

## LAC

ECUK table U2, generated by `baseline/build.py`. Cooking values are fuel input. Services catering gas is 11.38 TWh (gas plus oil), corrected from a hand derived 11.39. Services lighting and appliances electric is lighting plus computing plus the electric part of "other" (50.9 TWh); the gas value folds in the oil, bioenergy and heat sold parts of "other" (10.71 TWh), and catering and cooling oil is folded into gas, matching the 2018 workbook treatment.

## Space and water heating

Generated by `baseline/build.py` (`baseline/inputs/05-heating.json`). ECUK U2 fuel demands are converted to heat with the workbook efficiencies (gas and oil boilers 82.5%, solid fuel 75%, wood 70%, direct electric and heat sold 100%). Heat pump ambient heat from DUKES 6.1 is added, split between space and water in proportion to each sector's U2 electricity use: domestic 3.3 TWh, services 12.0 TWh (commercial, public administration, agriculture and miscellaneous) and industry 0.04 TWh, all industry ambient going to space heat. The large services figure reflects the revised DUKES methodology that counts reversible air conditioning. DUKES 6.1 solar thermal is added to water heating.

Heat demand targets: domestic space 203.8, services space 104.6, industry space 14.5, domestic water 57.61 and services water 15.76 TWh. Services water was 15.77 in an earlier hand derivation. The three GW/K values are calibrated against the model's space heat output for each sector and reproduce 322.9 TWh modelled space heat.

Heating system shares use the U2 total space and water heating rows across all three sectors (389.5 TWh). Heat pump heat is calibrated so the ambient heat drawn by the model's heat pumps equals DUKES 6.1 ambient heat (15.3 TWh), at the model's own seasonal efficiency rather than the workbook's SPF 3. That gives 23.4 TWh of heat pump heat. Direct electric is U2 heating electricity less the heat pumps' electricity. Shares: heat pumps 6.02%, direct electric 6.32% (were 5.89 and 6.45 with SPF 3). Heat sold and solar hot water are left out, which reallocates them in proportion. The oil (synthfuel) share rises to 11.7% because ECUK 2024 reports higher services oil heating than the 2018 vintage tables did. Hydrogen share and direct electric efficiencies are now set explicitly, with the same values as the ZCB default.

## Transport

Generated by `baseline/build.py` (`baseline/inputs/07-transport.json`). The model multiplies `miles_pp` by population, so miles per person must follow passenger kilometres per person. Cars, rail, bus and motorbike scale the 2018 baseline by TSGB0101 passenger kilometres (2024 over 2018, both from the current edition, which absorbs series revisions) and by population (2018 over 2024). Aviation uses AVI0201c on the same basis. An earlier version scaled road and rail by passenger kilometres only, which left out population growth and put those values about 4% high: cars 6049 (was 6303), rail 683 (712), bus 256 (267), motorbike 46 (48), aviation 3116. Walking and cycling from NTS0303 (England only, used as a UK proxy).

EV shares are miles based estimates: cars 5% (battery electric stock 3.8%, 1.29 million cars, driven above fleet average), bus 3.5% and motorbike 1% (licensed fleet shares from VEH1103). The rail electric share is calibrated so modelled rail electricity equals DUKES 1.1 rail electricity (5.08 TWh), giving 72.1% of passenger km (was the 2018 value of 66%). Diesel rail efficiency is calibrated to DUKES rail petroleum at the same time.

`efficiency.IC` is an engine efficiency divisor, not fuel per km. Each mode's value is calibrated against the model's fuel use for that mode, which is inversely proportional to it:

- Cars: DUKES 1.1 road petroleum plus bioenergy (37,800.6 ktoe) times the ECUK C8 car share of road fuel (0.612, from the 2023 row since C8 runs a year behind), 269.25 TWh, as in the 2018 workbook boundary. 0.2243 (was 0.24).
- Buses and motorcycles: the same road fuel times their C8 shares, 9.84 and 2.06 TWh. 0.3185 and 0.1244 (were the ZCB default 0.3 and 0.115).
- Diesel rail: DUKES 1.1 rail petroleum, 6.22 TWh. 0.3467 (was the ZCB default 0.3).
- Aviation: DUKES 1.1 air, 157.3 TWh including international departures and sustainable aviation fuel. 0.1818 (was the ZCB default 0.2). Passenger km are UK airlines only, so the calibration also absorbs that boundary difference.

Transport biofuel, 32.0 TWh (DUKES 1.1 transport bioenergy, road and aviation), is supplied to the liquid fuel store (`synth_fuel.biofuel_supply_TWhy`), so fossil oil falls by the same amount. Other transport constants (mechanical kWh per passenger km, electric and hydrogen efficiencies, aviation load factor, smart charging and V2G off) are set explicitly with the ZCB default values so a change to the default cannot leak in. Freight IC is DUKES road liquid fuel times the C8 HGV plus LGV share (0.360), plus DUKES national navigation, 167.8 TWh (was 172, which could not be reproduced from source). Modelled road fuel totals about 440 TWh against 439.6 TWh in DUKES. The EV battery store (77 GWh) and charge rate (9.0 GW) are the 1.29 million battery electric cars licensed at the end of 2024 (VEH1103) times an assumed 60 kWh battery and 7 kW charger.

## Industry

Generated by `baseline/build.py` (`baseline/inputs/08-industry.json`). ECUK table U4, total industries, in ktoe. Motors, compressed air, lighting and refrigeration are all electric in U4 and in the model. Fuel shares within each end use round gas, oil and coal to one decimal place and give electricity the remainder, so each end use sums to exactly 100. This moves high temperature process coal to 13.6% and electricity to 24.9% (were 13.5 and 25). U4 has no bioenergy column, so bioenergy shares are 0.

`other_heat_TWhy` is the U2 industry "unknown (heat)" row (27.6 TWh: heat sold 25.9% mapped to CH4, bioenergy 74.1%). `other_non_heat_TWhy` is the U4 "other" row plus the 1.4 TWh of electricity in the U2 "unknown (other)" row, 16.3 TWh, with fuel shares recalculated on that total. The rest of the "unknown (other)" row (23.7 TWh, mostly oil) has no 2018 counterpart and is carried in `fossil_fuels`. Modelled industry demand (191.7 TWh) less `refinery_electric_TWhy` (4.7), plus space heating fuel (16.2) and that 23.7 TWh, gives 226.9 TWh against the ECUK industry total of 227.0. Hydrogen and the unused `other_heat` fuel shares are set to 0 explicitly. `refinery_electric_TWhy` 4.7 TWh is DUKES 5.1 energy industry use less electricity generation and energy storage.

## Backup, energy industry use, fossil fuels

Generated by `baseline/build.py` (`baseline/inputs/09-electricity-fuels.json`). Shares of 2024 dispatchable generation (DUKES 5.6 annual summaries, all generating companies, 137.6 TWh excluding nuclear): gas 63.54%, bioenergy 29.17%, oil 1.27%, and coal plus other thermal as the remainder, 6.02%. Other thermal, mainly waste, is 4.5 of those 6.0 points. Efficiencies are generation over fuel used from 5.6: gas 48.7%, bioenergy 34.9%, oil 36.8% (previously the ZCB default of 21% applied), and coal plus other thermal together 24.7% (previously the coal only 41.7%, which understated fuel for this share). Its CO2 factor is power station CO2 from coal, municipal solid waste and other fuels over that fuel, 0.254 kgCO2/kWh (`coal_co2_factor`), in place of the coal factor of 0.317. DUKES 5.10 gives slightly different plant efficiencies. Grid losses 8.8% and generation own use 3.45% of total supply (DUKES 5.1).

`other_gas_use` 4.3 TWh is the U2 industry "unknown (other)" gas. It previously also included DUKES 4.1 gas losses, which the model calculates separately, so losses were counted twice. `other_oil_use` 19.3 TWh and `other_coal_use` 0.1 TWh are the oil and coal of that row.

Energy industry own use and gas losses are model inputs (`energy_industry_use.*_prc`), previously fixed 2018 constants in model.js. Each is calibrated so the model reproduces the DUKES 1.1 energy industry use row: refinery oil 5.18% and gas 0.759% of oil demand (33.5 and 4.9 TWh), extraction gas 3.149% and oil 0.4% of oil and gas demand (39.7 and 5.0 TWh), gas losses 0.778% of gas demand (4.8 TWh).

Electric storage power is 8.0 GW, pumped hydro plus grid batteries (DUKES 5.16). DUKES does not report energy capacity, so 31 GWh is about 24 GWh of pumped hydro plus 1.3 hours of battery storage. Charge and discharge efficiencies are each 0.89, the square root of the 79% round trip in DUKES 5.16. The model only charges storage from surplus supply, which a baseline rarely has, so modelled storage output is far below the 3.5 TWh in DUKES.

Fuel prices for the LCOE are those paid by major power producers (DESNZ QEP 3.2.1): gas 3.50, oil 5.25 and coal 1.40 p/kWh. Biomass and carbon prices are not included. Methane storage keeps the 2018 convention of 120 TWh (network plus supply flexibility); DUKES 4.4 storage capacity alone is 35.5 TWh.

## Emissions

Generated by `baseline/build.py` (`baseline/inputs/10-emissions.json`) from the 2024 by source dataset (sheet `UK_by_source`), with each field a sum over matching rows. The mapping follows the GHG sheet of the 2018 workbook:

- Fuel combustion categories contribute CH4 and N2O only, since the model calculates fuel CO2. This applies to power stations, residential, commercial, public, industrial and agricultural combustion and domestic transport. Municipal solid waste CO2 (7.07 Mt) is left to the model through the coal plus other thermal backup share.
- Oil and gas supply gives non CO2 plus non fuel CO2, less flaring and venting, which have their own field.
- Process, fugitive, product use, waste and agriculture categories contribute all gases.
- Land use is split by CRT code. Burning goes to `biomass_burning`, mineralisation N2O to `land_use_n2o`, and the rest of cropland, grassland, wetlands and settlements to their own fields. Forest land and harvested wood products (-20.2 MtCO2e) are left to the land use model.

Every 2024 territorial emission is either in one field or calculated by the model. An earlier hand derivation counted burning and mineralisation N2O twice by taking whole land use groups (1.46 MtCO2e), and left out combustion non CO2 from commercial, public, industrial and agricultural combustion, lubricants and urea, which the 2018 workbook included. Net, the emissions balance is 0.49 MtCO2e lower. The territorial total is 373.4 MtCO2e and 414.6 including international aviation and shipping.

Other changes since 2018 are methodological as well as real: AR5 GWPs, explicit peatland reporting, wastewater now includes effluent N2O, and ammonia production ceased in 2023, so `ammonia` is 0. Land use areas keep the 2018 values; the modelled forest sink plus harvested wood (-21 MtCO2e) approximates the inventory -20.2.

## Validation

`node docs/run_scenario.js baseline_2024` prints:

```
Electricity demand (TWh/yr): 306.7
Onshore wind CF: 24.5%
Offshore wind CF: 34.5%
Solar PV CF: 8.8%
Space heating demand (TWh/yr): 322.9
Water heating demand (TWh/yr): 73.4
Industry demand (TWh/yr): 191.7
Total emissions (MtCO2e): 436.0
Grid CO2 intensity (gCO2/kWh): 130.1
```

"Electricity demand" is total generation before grid losses, including imports and backup. It is 306.7 TWh against 319.4 TWh total electricity supply in DUKES 5.1, about 4% low, mostly because modelled backup is 9 TWh below observed. Grid CO2 intensity is fossil backup CO2 over that total, 130.1 gCO2/kWh. LCOE including fuel is 65.3 £/MWh. Wind and solar capacity factors reproduce DUKES 6.2 generation. Heating and industry match the ECUK derivations above.

Total emissions are 436.0 MtCO2e. The statistics give 414.6 including international aviation and shipping, and the model adds an aviation non CO2 uplift of 29.0, so the like for like comparison is 443.6. The model is 1.7% low. The main changes from the earlier 449.4 are imports replacing gas backup, transport biofuel replacing fossil oil, the gas losses double count, aviation fuel now calibrated to DUKES, and the lower CO2 factor for coal and waste backup.
