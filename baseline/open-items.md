# Open items

Items found while building and validating baselines that need further attention. Remove an item once it is resolved. `baseline/validate.py` refers to these sections from checks that fail for a known reason (see [reports/validation_baseline_2024.md](reports/validation_baseline_2024.md)).

## Model

- **run.js fails.** It stops with `normalise_profile is not defined`, as it does not define the function before loading defaults.js. docs/run_scenario.js does the same job and works.
- **Storage cycles less than real storage.** The optimal dispatch charges from backup to shave backup peaks, giving 0.96 TWh in 2024 and 0.25 TWh in 2018 against 3.5 and 2.5 TWh in DUKES 5.16. Real storage also arbitrages against prices, which the model does not include. A higher `electric_storage.peak_weight` raises output (3.2 TWh at 50 in 2024) but has no source. The build reports this as a failed `check` on `electric_storage.capacity_GWh`.
- **Baseline biomass fuel uses a forward pellet price, and 2018 has no carbon price.** Fuel prices for gas, oil and coal are from DESNZ QEP 3.2.1. The 2024 carbon price is the mean UK ETS auction price (Argus) plus Carbon Price Support, entered as published values. 2018 keeps 0 until an EU ETS series is sourced. There is no official series for biomass fuel prices paid by generators, so `fuel_prices.biomass` uses the Argus forward pellet price for 2026 (£39.8/MWh, see docs/cost-assumptions.md).
- **Baseline LCOE values the existing fleet at 2024 new-build costs** (see [docs/cost-assumptions.md](../docs/cost-assumptions.md)). Existing nuclear is costed at Sizewell C prices. Imports and biomass plant capital are not costed, and hydro uses new-build run of river costs.
- **Biofuel supply is flat.** It feeds the liquid fuel store, so the profile has little effect.
- **The build-up is 14% below what households paid for generation in 2024.** The build-up for every technology (the 2024 fleet at new-build costs, with 2024 fuel and carbon prices, and biomass at the forward pellet price) is 14.1 p/kWh delivered, against 16.4 p/kWh for wholesale, capacity market, RO and FiT in the 2024 Ofgem cap. 2024 generation prices close the gap in two steps: generation at 2024 capture prices plus £12.8bn of support gives 13.6 p/kWh, and the domestic wholesale adjustment (3.2 p/kWh, forward purchasing in 2023 and the domestic profile) gives 16.8. Retail prices use the generation prices. The build-up check stays as a known difference between cost and price. The implied hurdle rates in [docs/cost-assumptions.md](../docs/cost-assumptions.md) show the difference by technology.
- **Gas build-up is 57% above the 2024 gas price.** At 2024 fuel and carbon prices and the DUKES fleet efficiency, fuel and carbon alone are about £93/MWh against the £85 capture price, and turbine capacity at new-build cost, de-rated for outages, adds £40/MWh. The fleet is largely paid off and earns capacity market and balancing payments, which the capture price leaves out. The 2024 baseline keeps thermal plant on 2024 prices. Capacity market payments sit in `other_support_gbp_bn`, so they would be counted twice if thermal plant moved to the build-up.
- **Generation price inputs entered as published values.** Capture prices (LCCC day-ahead price weighted by Elexon FUELHH and NESO embedded generation) and CfD payments by technology (LCCC daily data summed over 2024) were derived from API series and entered with a citation. A script that fetches and derives them would make them reproducible. Scheme years (RO, FiT) and delivery years (capacity market) are weighted to calendar 2024 by quarters. Onshore wind uses the transmission wind capture price, coal and waste use the CCGT capture price, and imports the demand weighted price.
- **ZCB generation cost uses AR7 strike prices.** Offshore wind £91.20, onshore £72.24, solar £65.23, tidal £265, nuclear £124.7 and hydro £138/MWh (the hydro build-up at 35%, as hydro has no CfD) (2024 prices, [docs/cost-assumptions.md](../docs/cost-assumptions.md)), with backup, storage and other plant on the build-up. This gives 18.9 p/kWh against 20.1 with the build-up for every technology, because strike prices assume higher load factors than the model's weather data gives. Strike prices pay for metered output, but the model's output is before curtailment, and curtailment and balancing payments are not included. Network costs are kept at 2024 levels.
- **Storage in the 2024 baseline uses the build-up.** Modelled storage output is low, so its build-up is £917/MWh of output (£0.88bn), with pumped hydro at battery costs. Real storage earns an arbitrage spread, which needs charge and discharge prices.
- **One heating system mix for all sectors.** Shares come from the all sector ECUK total, so the model gives homes less gas (230 against 256 TWh) and services more (125 against 109 TWh) than DUKES, and homes 6% more electricity. Sector specific shares would fix this.
- **Harvested wood products are credited at harvest.** The model counts 95% of harvested carbon as stored (3.0 MtCO2 in 2024). The inventory reports the net change in the wood products pool, 2.2 MtCO2, which subtracts the decay of older products. A pool with a decay rate would fix this, and matters for ZCB scenarios that rely on timber.
- **ZCB land model defaults keep the old behaviour.** `land_model` in zcb_2024.json credits no existing woodland sink (`existing_woodland_tCO2_per_ha` 0), grows all direct use biomass on UK land (`uk_crop_biomass_prc` 100), keeps 1.29 MtCO2 of landfill biochar and the 24,728 kha UK area. The 2024 baseline sets 5.47 tCO2/ha, 0.45%, 0 and 24,417 kha. Whether ZCB should credit the existing forest sink, how much of its biomass is imported, and whether landfill biochar is a real option are scenario decisions.
- **Peak demand is high.** The highest modelled hour is 55.2 GW against a 2024 simultaneous maximum load of 47.4 GW (DUKES 5.10). The model peak covers ten weather years and includes demand met by embedded generation, but it is above every year since 2010, which suggests the space heat profile is too peaky.
- **Backup generation is 6% low.** Gas, bioenergy and coal plus other generation are each about 6.4% below DUKES 5.6, because modelled electricity demand is 2.4% low at final consumption.
- **FT takes all of the hydrogen surplus above the liquid store target.** FT tops up from the H2 store while the liquid store is below `synth_fuel.store_target_GWh`, but above the target it still takes the whole same hour surplus up to capacity. Before shipping was added, the zcb_2024 liquid store rose from 5 to 16.6 TWh over the ten years. When the top-up runs it competes with methanation for the same store: at the 2010 PyPSA expansion capacities it removes 8.4 TWh/yr of unmet synthetic fuel and takes the methane store to empty. The split of hydrogen between FT, methanation and the H2 store is set by capacities and priority order, which makes it hard to tune.
- **Marginal values and production costs differ.** Fuel users pay production cost, but `fuel_values` still set hourly electricity prices. In zcb_2024 methane costs £159/MWh to make against a marginal value of £235. Hydrogen and methane stores count fully in the fuel, although they also serve turbines.
- **Fuel values are provisional.** `fuel_values` come from one year (2010) of a PyPSA expansion at 3 hour steps, and use one methane value for turbines and end users. Over ten years the hourly prices recover 85% of zcb_2024 system cost. Phase 6 recalibrates them.
- **Network cost is an estimate from national totals.** zcb_2024 has £24.7bn/yr: the 2024/25 network revenue from all users (£14.3bn), distribution at £980/kW of peak above 2024 (£2.6bn) and transmission at £1,100/kW of wind above 2024 (£7.9bn). Unit costs come from DESNZ and Ofgem 2022 modelling and NESO investment totals, not from engineering costs. The transmission part of the 2024/25 revenue is a forecast, and the Northern Ireland part is from a press report. Update them when ED3 business plans are published (due December 2026). See [docs/cost-assumptions.md](../docs/cost-assumptions.md).
- **Solar export does not drive distribution.** Distribution is sized on peak import. The model does not split rooftop from ground mounted solar, so midday export on low voltage networks is not costed.
- **Transmission is not tested against a zonal model.** zcb_2024 adds no constraint cost above 2024, on the basis that transmission at £1,100/kW of wind is built for the wind and curtailment is already costed. Solar, nuclear, interconnectors and demand growth add no transmission. A zonal check in ZCB_PyPSA, with network and curtailment costed together, would test whether £1,100/kW buys enough boundary capacity.
- **Strike prices are paid on curtailed output.** Generation cost uses output before curtailment, so about 108 TWh of curtailed electricity costs about £9bn/yr at the mean strike price in zcb_2024. Real CfDs pay on metered output, with curtailment compensated separately.
- **Baseline hourly end use is above final consumption.** In baseline_2024, hourly end use is 269.1 TWh and final electricity consumption 264.4 TWh. They match in zcb_2024. The cause has not been traced.
- **Final bioenergy is 13% high.** Bioenergy, ambient and solar heat in final consumption is 65.5 TWh against 57.8 TWh. The cause has not been traced.

## Cost model

Found in a review of cost_model.js on 2026-11-07. Figures are zcb_2024 and baseline_2024.

- **Demand side costs are missing.** Total cost covers energy supply and networks. It leaves out building retrofit, heat pumps, boilers, heat network connections, vehicles and modal shift. zcb_2024 relies on these to cut final energy from 1,507 to 689 TWh, so its comparison with the baseline is favourable and total cost is not yet a cost to society.
- **Non-domestic standing charges are missing** from the cost to consumers on the per kWh network basis, which no preset uses now. That basis gives £12.5bn for 2024 against £14.3bn of 2024/25 network revenue from users.
- **The domestic unit rate carries the whole domestic network cost.** Real network charges are now largely fixed per site, so the model overstates the network cost of one more kWh at home. Running cost comparisons that use the unit rate, such as heat pump against gas boiler or car charging against petrol, should take the network part out. Electric car charging is keyed as other use for the network but priced at the household unit rate in household totals.
- **The social discount rate reaches energy supply only.** Network, supplier, import and fossil fuel prices keep their private returns in the cost to society, including £24.7bn/yr of networks in zcb_2024 and £12.5bn/yr in the baseline. This slightly overstates zcb_2024. The rate also has no optimism bias adjustment, so the total is shown as a range.
- **Generator network charges overlap with retail network charges.** Generator `fixed_om` and strike prices include connection and use of system charges, which also sit in retail network charges. The overlap has not been sized.
- **Hydrogen delivery uses the gas figure.** Hydrogen needs its own pipes and filling stations, so its £18.8/MWh is likely to be low. It needs a sourced figure.
- **Rooftop solar thermal cost is a midpoint.** £1,620/kWth (£252/MWh of heat) sits between the UK MCS figure (£5,877 per install, about £320/MWh for a 4 m² system) and measured European prices (about £200/MWh). The MCS kW basis and average system size are not confirmed, and the dashboard returned an error. The Danish Energy Agency cost (£108/MWh) is below measured Danish prices. In zcb_2024 rooftop costs £3.1bn/yr against £0.5bn/yr for large arrays, so the 50/50 split (a working assumption) still decides the heat cost.
- **Heat network losses and pumping are not in the energy balance.** Large solar and geothermal heat reach buildings without loss. The Danish Energy Agency suburban network loses 12.4% of heat and uses electricity at 2% of heat delivered. Service pipes and building substations are not costed, as with heat pumps and boilers.
- **Wood fuel prices are proxies.** Home wood uses Nottingham Energy Partnership prices from 2025 to 2026, per kWh net against gross model volumes (about 10% high). Bulk wood uses the Argus forward pellet price for 2026, and some industrial bioenergy is cheaper waste.
- **Exchange rates are recalled figures.** £0.89/€ (2020, solar district heating), £0.85/€ (2025, heat networks), $1.25/£ (Ember's pellet conversion) and $1.278/£ (2024, jet fuel and marine gas oil) are not checked against Bank of England data.
- **The carbon value is the 2024 value.** A scenario for a later year should use a higher value (£280/tCO2e in 2030 and £378 in 2050, 2020 prices). The low and high series (half and one and a half times) are not shown.
- **Household totals are low estimates.** They include company cars, price all charging at the household unit rate and 5% VAT (public charging is dearer and pays 20%), and leave out solar thermal panels and other equipment.

## Industry

- **High temperature process coal is mostly a reductant.** Coke and coal in blast furnaces cannot be replaced by heat. Steel, cement, ammonia and glass need material based sub models.
- **Blast furnace fuel is a fixed input.** `industry.blast_furnace_coal_TWhy` (12.5 TWh in 2024, 23.9 in 2018) carries DUKES coke oven and blast furnace fuel as energy industry use, with a CO2 factor taken from the inventory (0.38 kgCO2/kWh). It does not respond to steel output or to the industry levers. ZCB sets it to 0. A steel sub model should replace it.
- **Drying fuel efficiency (65%) has no source.** The electric to fuel ratios come from JRC-IDEES 2021, which is EU data. The sources quote net calorific values and ECUK is gross.
- **Industry demand is flat.** Heat end uses use the flat high temperature profile and demand side response switches fuel instantly, with no heater capacity or thermal storage.

## Inputs

- **DUKES 6.1 commercial ambient heat** (12.0 TWh) includes reversible air conditioning, which inflates the heat pump share.
- **Boiler and heat pump efficiencies** are carried from the 2018 baseline.
- **Assumed per unit values:** EV battery 60 kWh and charger 7 kW per car, car EV miles at 1.32 times the battery electric stock share, battery storage duration 1.3 hours, pumped hydro energy 24 GWh.
- **Interconnector capacity** sums the interconnectors listed in DUKES 5.13A by commissioning year. The table lists current links only, so a closed link would be missed for a past year.
- **Land use areas with no cached source.** Urban (1,459 kha), coastal and freshwater (692) and semi natural grassland (155) are ZCB values, and mountain, heath and bog is the remainder of the UK area (2,080 kha), so it carries their error. The natural and productive woodland split keeps the ZCB proportions. Woodland area is a published value from the Forest Research PDF.
- **baseline_2018 keeps the old land use.** The land inputs are limited to 2024 because woodland area is a 2024 published value. The 2018 baseline still has 1,475 kha of new woodland standing in for the forest sink and ZCB areas elsewhere.
- **Gas households exclude Northern Ireland.** The share of households with gas is GB domestic meters over UK households (86.3% in 2024).
- **baseline_2018 miles per person are anchored to the original 2018 values.** The transport formulas scale the original 2018 baseline by passenger km and population, so the rebuilt 2018 values are the original ones with revisions applied, not an independent derivation.

## Build

- **Households source URL is not versioned.** ONS only offers a `current` link for families and households, so the file will change when the 2026 edition is published. The hash check will catch it.
- **NTS0303 covers England only.** Walking and cycling `miles_pp` use it as a UK proxy, as in the 2018 baseline.
- **Ofgem refuses scripted downloads.** `ofgem_cap` is marked `manual` in `sources.json` and has to be downloaded in a browser.
- **Some sources are 2024 only.** The Ofgem cap model is read by cell range, woodland area is a published value and the DESNZ conversion factors file is the 2024 edition. Those inputs and checks apply to 2024 only, and baseline_2018 takes the archived default for them (the old single oil CO2 factor among them).

## Observed data

- **International shipping fuel differs between DUKES and the inventory.** DUKES 1.1 marine bunkers (23.9 TWh in 2024) give 5.8 MtCO2 at the marine gas oil factor. The inventory estimates shipping from vessel activity and gives 5.0 MtCO2. The baseline follows DUKES so the energy balance holds.
- **ONS household energy spend is below DUKES use at QEP prices.** For 2024, ONS gives £26.3bn for electricity and £14.6bn for gas, against £30.1bn and £19.2bn from DUKES domestic use at DESNZ average prices. Both are reported, and the DUKES based figure is the primary check.
